# 24 and 40 are the only Δ-primitive numbers with exactly two prime factors

**Proved.** The conjecture as stated in its source.

This folder is a self-contained Lean project in the layout the
[Palomar registry](https://palomar-registry.org/) asks for: the full statement and the
full proof are here, and nothing imports or depends on another repository. The result
was first published, with the checker's output, at
[`conjectures/delta_primitive_two_primes/`](https://github.com/marcosmartinsalvador/conjecture-proofs/tree/a051c72158ef471e5541f0ddad2435755fbb070c/conjectures/delta_primitive_two_primes).

## What is proved

A number n has the "Δ property" when one difference of two divisors that multiply to n
is the sum of two such differences (for 24: 12 − 2 = 10 = 5 + 5, where 5 = 8 − 3). It is
"Δ-primitive" when it has the property and is not a square, at least 4, times a smaller
number with the property.

**Proved here:** among the numbers p^x q^y built from two different primes, with
x, y ≥ 1, only 24 and 40 are Δ-primitive. The source proves the case y = 1.

**Not treated:** numbers with three or more prime factors.

## The statement as printed

V. N. Schvöllner, "The Δ property: a bridge between split graphs and Number Theory",
arXiv:2605.25264v1 (24 May 2026). The conjecture is already in the author's 2025 thesis
(arXiv:2507.13479).

> Based on experimental evidence, we conjecture that 24 and 40 are the only
> $\Delta$-primitive numbers using exactly two primes in their decomposition.
>
> Let $n$ be a natural number of the form $p^x q^y$, where $p$ and $q$ are distinct
> primes and $x, y\in\mathbb{N}$. If $n$ is $\Delta$-primitive, then $n \in \{24, 40\}$.

## How the Lean statement reads it

[`Challenge.lean`](Challenge.lean) follows the paper's definitions word for word:

- `Dstar n` is the set of differences |a − b| over pairs of natural numbers with
  a · b = n. The difference is taken in the integers.
- `Dplus n` is the set of sums x + y with x, y in `Dstar n`, both nonzero. They may be
  equal, as the paper's own examples 24 and 40 need.
- `HasDelta n` says the two sets meet. `DeltaPrimitive n` says that n has the property
  and is not α²m with α, m ≥ 2 and m having the property.
- `Claim`: for primes p ≠ q and x, y ≥ 1, if p^x q^y is Δ-primitive then it is 24 or 40.
  The compared theorem is `result : Claim`.

The exponents are at least 1 because the paper's natural numbers start at 1. The thesis
words the conjecture with x, y ≥ 0; the extra cases (n = 1 and prime powers) are not
formalized here. The paper proves that those numbers never have the Δ property.

## Idea of the proof

The Δ property gives three pairs of complementary divisors whose differences satisfy
"one is the sum of the other two". If a prime divides all six numbers, dividing n by its
square shows that n is not primitive. So each of p and q divides both members of at
most one pair, the pair (1, n) cannot occur, and the few shapes that are left force n to
be 24, 40 or 216. And 216 = 3² · 24 is not primitive.

The full argument on paper, in seven steps, is in
[`PROOF_NOTES.md`](https://github.com/marcosmartinsalvador/conjecture-proofs/blob/a051c72158ef471e5541f0ddad2435755fbb070c/conjectures/delta_primitive_two_primes/PROOF_NOTES.md) in the
result's own folder; the proof in [`Solution.lean`](Solution.lean) follows those steps.

## Is it new?

**Fairly sure.** Nothing was found on arXiv, on the web or on GitHub: no proof, and no
earlier formalization. The paper has only one version, and the author's later papers do
not treat this conjecture. Another public group started work on the author's follow-up
paper the day before this proof was checked.

Not searched: citation indexes and journal databases (MathSciNet, zbMATH, Google
Scholar). No search can show that nobody did this before.
