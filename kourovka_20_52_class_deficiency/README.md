# Kourovka problem 20.52: a partial answer

**Partial result. The problem is still open.**

This folder is a self-contained Lean project in the layout the
[Palomar registry](https://palomar-registry.org/) asks for: the full statement and the
full proof are here, and nothing imports or depends on another repository. The result
was first published, with the checker's output, at
[`conjectures/kourovka_20_52_class_deficiency/`](https://github.com/marcosmartinsalvador/conjecture-proofs/tree/a051c72158ef471e5541f0ddad2435755fbb070c/conjectures/kourovka_20_52_class_deficiency).

## What is proved

For a finite group G of odd order, the number of elements minus the number k(G) of
conjugacy classes is always a multiple of 16 (Burnside). Problem 20.52 of the Kourovka
Notebook asks whether every multiple 16m, m > 0, is reached by some non-abelian group
of odd order.

**Proved here:** yes for every odd m, and for every m in which 2 occurs to an odd power
(m = 2, 6, 8, 10, 14, 18, 22, 24, ...). That is five sixths of all m, by density.

**Not proved:** anything for m = 4, 12, 16, 20, 28, ... (2 to an even power, at least
2). The problem is not solved.

## The statement as printed

*Unsolved Problems in Group Theory. The Kourovka Notebook*, No. 21, edited by
E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov, arXiv:1401.0300v50 (November 2026), problem 20.52,
posed by D. MacHale:

> **20.52.** A famous result of Burnside states that if $k(G)$ is the number of
> conjugacy classes of a finite group $G$ of odd order, then $|G|- k(G)$ is
> divisible by 16. Is it true that for every integer $m > 0$ there exists a
> finite non-abelian group $G(m)$ of odd order such that
> $|G(m)| - k(G(m)) = 16m$? *D. MacHale*

## How the Lean statement reads it

[`Challenge.lean`](Challenge.lean) defines `Attained m`: there is a type `G` with a group
structure, finite, with `Nat.card G` odd, with two elements that do not commute, and
with `Nat.card G = Nat.card (ConjClasses G) + 16 * m`. `Claim` says that every `m > 0`
that is odd, or with `padicValNat 2 m` odd, is attained. The compared theorem is
`result : Claim`.

- `Claim` is **not** the problem. The problem asks for every m > 0; `Claim` covers odd
  m and m in which 2 occurs to an odd power.
- "Non-abelian" is `∃ x y, x * y ≠ y * x`. "Odd order" is `Finite G` with
  `Odd (Nat.card G)`. k(G) is `Nat.card (ConjClasses G)`, Mathlib's type of conjugacy
  classes. The equation is written with `+`; this is the same, because k(G) ≤ |G|.
- `G : Type` (the lowest universe) is no loss: every finite group has a copy there, and
  the claim is an existence statement.
- `padicValNat 2 m` is the exponent of 2 in m.

## Idea of the proof

Write m = 2^j a with a odd. If a group G works for m, then G × Z/a' works for m a'
(a' odd), so the question is whether 2^(j+4) has the form |G| − k(G) for every j ≥ 0.

Take an odd n prime to 3 and a residue u modulo n with u² + u + 1 = 0. Then 1 − u and
1 − u² are units (their product is 3). Let Z/3 act on Z/n by multiplication by u and
form G = (Z/n ⋊ Z/3) × Z/a with a odd. Counting commuting pairs gives a²(n² + 8n), so
3 k(G) = a(n + 8), and

|G| − k(G) = 3an − a(n + 8)/3 = 8a(n − 1)/3.

- n = 7, u = 2 gives 16a: every odd m.
- n = 12y² + 1, u = 6y² + 3y with y = 2^i gives 32ay² = 16 · 2^(2i+1) · a: every m in
  which 2 occurs to the odd power 2i + 1.

In [`Solution.lean`](Solution.lean): Part 1 builds the group on triples (x, y, s),
Part 2 counts the commuting pairs (`card_comm`) and uses Mathlib's
`card_comm_eq_card_conjClasses_mul_card`, Part 3 (`attained_of`, `result`) puts in the
two choices of n and u.

## Is it new?

**Fairly sure, but the step is short.** The question comes from S. M. Buckley and
D. MacHale, "Conjugate deficiency in finite groups", Irish Math. Soc. Bulletin 71
(2013), 13 to 19, Problem 5. That paper was read in full. It already gives a group for
each of 2^4, ..., 2^12, by nine single examples, which settles every m in which 2
occurs at most 8 times, so in particular every m below 512. Nothing here is new in that
range. Four of those nine examples are the first groups of the family used here, but
the paper says it sees no clear enough pattern, and does not have the family. What is
added here is that pattern: one proof for all odd powers of two, with first new value
m = 512.

Another public project, `JUrban/kour1`, read the 2013 paper, checked every group of odd
order up to 1727 and found every m ≤ 64. Its notes describe the same kind of group for
m = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 and say that the general question remains unresolved there. No
other public project was found with an answer, and no earlier formalization.

Not searched: papers that cite the 2013 paper, MathSciNet and zbMATH. No search can
show that nobody did this before.
